Motorola intrinsically safe radios: every ATEX & IECEx certified model compared

Motorola Solutions is the dominant vendor in the intrinsically safe two-way radio market. This guide covers every current Motorola model with ATEX or IECEx certification, explains the certification differences between variants, and identifies the most common accessory certification mistakes.

ATEX vs IECEx vs UL certification variants

Motorola offers region-specific certification variants of the same hardware. Understanding which certificate applies to your market avoids procurement delays and compliance issues.

ATEX (Directive 2014/34/EU) is mandatory for the EU/EEA market. The ATEX-certified DP4401Ex and DP4801Ex carry a CE mark with the ATEX hexagon symbol and a four-digit Notified Body number. This is the variant most commonly stocked by European distributors. For a full explanation of the directive's scope and conformity requirements, see the ATEX Directive 2014/34/EU reference on exknowledge.com.

IECEx is the international certification system accepted in most countries outside the EU — including Australia, Singapore, Brazil, and parts of the Middle East. The IECEx certificate references the same IEC 60079 standards as ATEX. Many Motorola Ex radios carry dual ATEX + IECEx certification, allowing deployment across both EU and international sites without separate procurement.

UL (Class I, Division 1) applies to the North American market, where the NEC Division system is used instead of IEC zones. The UL-certified variants are electrically identical but carry different labels and documentation. A radio certified to Class I, Division 1 can be used in Division 1 and Division 2 areas. If your site operates under both IEC and NEC classification systems, dual-certified radios simplify fleet management.

Accessory certification traps

The most common compliance failure with Motorola intrinsically safe radios is using non-certified accessories. Every component that enters the hazardous area — battery, antenna, speaker-microphone, earpiece, carry case — must be listed on the radio's Ex certificate schedule.

Batteries. The DP4401Ex and DP4801Ex use dedicated IMPRES Ex battery packs (PMNN4467, PMNN4468). Standard IMPRES batteries from non-Ex Motorola radios are physically compatible but not certified — using one invalidates the radio's Ex protection. The Ex battery packs have a lower capacity than standard IMPRES batteries because the intrinsic safety barrier limits charging and discharging energy.

Speaker-microphones. Motorola's PMMN4106 remote speaker-microphone is the certified accessory for hazardous area use. Third-party speaker-microphones — even those marketed as "Ex compatible" — are not covered by Motorola's certificate unless explicitly listed. The PMMN4106 has its own Ex marking and is assessed as associated apparatus.

Charging. IMPRES Ex chargers are designed for safe-area use only. The standard practice is to remove the radio from the hazardous area before placing it in the charger. Some sites install Zone 2-rated multi-unit chargers in transition areas — confirm the charger carries its own Ex ec or Ex nA certification for Zone 2 if this is your approach.

FAQ

Which Motorola radios are intrinsically safe?

Motorola Solutions offers four ATEX/IECEx certified intrinsically safe radios: the DP4401Ex and DP4801Ex (DMR, Zone 1), and their UL Class I Division 1 equivalents. All carry Ex ib IIC T4 Gb certification covering Zone 1 and Zone 2 for all gas groups.

Can I use standard Motorola accessories with an Ex-certified radio?

No. Only accessories listed on the radio's ATEX or IECEx certificate schedule may be used in the hazardous area. Using a standard Motorola earpiece or battery with a DP4401Ex invalidates the entire system's Ex certification.

What is the difference between DP4401Ex and DP4801Ex?

The DP4801Ex adds a full keypad and larger display. Both share the same Ex certification, DMR capability, IP68 rating, GPS, and AES-256 encryption. The DP4801Ex is preferred when users need to enter channel numbers or text manually.
