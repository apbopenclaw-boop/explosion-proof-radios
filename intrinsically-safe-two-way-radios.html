Intrinsically safe two-way radios: what they are and how to choose one

An intrinsically safe two-way radio is a portable communication device certified for use in explosive atmospheres. This guide explains what makes a two-way radio intrinsically safe, which certifications to look for, and how to select the right model for your hazardous area.

What makes a two-way radio intrinsically safe

Intrinsic safety (Ex i) is a protection concept defined in IEC 60079-11. It works by limiting the electrical and thermal energy in every circuit of the radio so that any spark or hot surface is too weak to ignite the surrounding explosive atmosphere. Unlike flameproof (Ex d) protection — which contains an explosion inside a heavy enclosure — intrinsic safety prevents ignition from happening in the first place.

For a two-way radio, this means the battery's maximum discharge current, the antenna's radiated power, and the speaker-microphone's electrical connections are all constrained to levels proven safe in testing. A Notified Body (for ATEX) or ExTL (for IECEx) evaluates the complete system — radio body, battery pack, antenna, and all accessories — as a single certified unit. Detailed information on how intrinsic safety compares to other Ex protection methods is available on exknowledge.com.

The practical result is that intrinsically safe two-way radios are lighter and more portable than flameproof alternatives, but have slightly lower transmit power and battery capacity. For handheld use in Zone 1 and Zone 2, intrinsic safety is the dominant and preferred protection concept worldwide.

How to choose an intrinsically safe two-way radio

Beyond matching the certification to your zone and gas group, several practical factors determine which intrinsically safe two-way radio is right for your operation:

Protocol. DMR (Digital Mobile Radio) is the current industry standard, offering AES-256 encryption, GPS tracking, text messaging, and doubled channel capacity via TDMA. Analogue radios are cheaper but lack encryption and are being phased out by most vendors. NXDN (Kenwood/Icom) and dPMR are niche alternatives with specific vendor ecosystems.

Gas group and temperature class. Most intrinsically safe two-way radios are certified to Ex ib IIC T4 Gb — this covers all gas groups (including hydrogen) and temperature classes down to 135 °C auto-ignition. If your site has only IIA gases (methane, propane), you could use IIA-rated equipment, but IIC radios provide universal coverage with no cost penalty.

Battery life. Intrinsically safe batteries have lower capacity than their standard counterparts because the Ex certification limits maximum energy. The radios on this site are rated for about 14 to 28 hours, depending on model and transmit-to-listen ratio. For 12-hour shifts, select a radio with at least 14 hours rated battery life to account for GPS and encryption overhead.

Existing infrastructure. If your site already runs a MOTOTRBO repeater network, Motorola radios integrate without additional infrastructure. The same applies to Kenwood NEXEDGE and TETRA systems. FAQ

What is an intrinsically safe two-way radio?

An intrinsically safe two-way radio is a portable communication device certified to operate in explosive atmospheres without causing ignition. It limits electrical and thermal energy in all circuits so that no spark or hot surface can ignite surrounding flammable gases or dust.

Do all hazardous areas require intrinsically safe radios?

No. Only Zone 0, 1, and 2 (gas) or Zone 20, 21, 22 (dust) require certified equipment. Zone 2 permits alternative protection concepts like Ex nA. Zone 1 specifically requires intrinsic safety or equivalent high-integrity protection.

How long do intrinsically safe radio batteries last?

The radios on this site are rated for about 14 to 28 hours on a single charge, depending on model, transmit duty cycle and GPS usage. Ex-certified batteries have lower capacity than standard batteries due to intrinsic safety energy limits.
